# eBay Inc. v. MercExchange, L.L.C.

**Citation:** 547 U.S. 388 (2006)
**Year:** 2006
**Court:** Supreme Court of the United States
**Topic Area:** Intellectual Property / Technology

## Facts
MercExchange held a patent on an electronic market for facilitating the sale of goods between private individuals using a central authority. MercExchange had previously tried to license the patent to eBay but negotiations failed. MercExchange sued eBay for patent infringement. A jury found infringement and awarded damages, but the district court denied MercExchange's request for a permanent injunction. The Federal Circuit reversed, applying its general rule that injunctions should issue upon a finding of infringement.

## Issue
Whether a permanent injunction should automatically issue upon a finding of patent infringement, or whether the traditional four-factor equitable test for injunctive relief applies.

## Holding
The Court held unanimously that the traditional four-factor test for permanent injunctions applies in patent cases. A plaintiff must demonstrate: (1) irreparable injury; (2) inadequate legal remedies; (3) the balance of hardships favors an injunction; and (4) the public interest would not be disserved. Neither the district court's categorical denial nor the Federal Circuit's general rule favoring injunctions was correct. The decision must be made on a case-by-case basis.

## Significance
eBay v. MercExchange fundamentally changed patent litigation strategy by eliminating the near-automatic right to injunctive relief for patent holders. The decision weakened the leverage of non-practicing entities (patent trolls) who used the threat of injunctions to extract large settlements. It shifted the patent enforcement landscape by making damages, rather than injunctions, the more common remedy in many patent cases.

## Key Quotes
> "The decision whether to grant or deny injunctive relief rests within the equitable discretion of the district courts, and that such discretion must be exercised consistent with traditional principles of equity."
