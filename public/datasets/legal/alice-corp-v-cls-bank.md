# Alice Corp. v. CLS Bank International

**Citation:** 573 U.S. 208 (2014)
**Year:** 2014
**Court:** Supreme Court of the United States
**Topic Area:** Intellectual Property / Technology

## Facts
Alice Corporation held patents on a computer-implemented scheme for mitigating settlement risk in financial transactions. The system used a computer as a third-party intermediary to facilitate the exchange of financial obligations between two parties. CLS Bank filed a declaratory judgment action arguing the patents were invalid because they claimed unpatentable abstract ideas.

## Issue
Whether claims directed to a computer-implemented scheme for mitigating settlement risk are patent-eligible under 35 U.S.C. Section 101, or whether they are drawn to an unpatentable abstract idea.

## Holding
The Court held unanimously that Alice Corporation's patents were invalid because they were drawn to the abstract idea of intermediated settlement and merely required generic computer implementation. Applying the two-step framework from Mayo v. Prometheus, the Court found that the claims did not contain an "inventive concept" sufficient to transform the abstract idea into a patent-eligible application. Simply implementing an abstract idea on a generic computer does not make it patentable.

## Significance
Alice established a rigorous test for patent eligibility that has had sweeping effects on software and business method patents. The two-step Alice/Mayo framework has been used to invalidate thousands of patents, particularly in the software industry. Critics argue the test creates uncertainty, while proponents say it eliminated low-quality patents that hindered innovation.

## Key Quotes
> "Merely requiring generic computer implementation fails to transform that abstract idea into a patent-eligible invention."
