# Mayo Collaborative Services v. Prometheus Laboratories, Inc.

**Citation:** 566 U.S. 66 (2012)
**Year:** 2012
**Court:** Supreme Court of the United States
**Topic Area:** Intellectual Property / Technology

## Facts
Prometheus Laboratories held patents on methods for determining the proper dosage of thiopurine drugs used to treat autoimmune diseases. The patents claimed the process of administering the drug, measuring metabolite levels in the patient's blood, and using the results to adjust dosing. Mayo Collaborative Services used a similar process and was sued for infringement.

## Issue
Whether claims that set forth laws of nature — specifically the correlations between blood test results and the likelihood of drug efficacy or toxicity — are patent-eligible under 35 U.S.C. Section 101.

## Holding
The Court held unanimously that Prometheus's patents were invalid because they effectively claimed laws of nature. The correlations between metabolite levels and drug efficacy are natural phenomena. The additional steps of administering the drug and measuring metabolites were well-understood, routine, and conventional activities that did not add enough to transform the nature of the claims into patent-eligible applications.

## Significance
Mayo established the foundational two-step framework for evaluating patent eligibility that was later applied in Alice Corp. v. CLS Bank. The decision significantly raised the bar for patenting methods based on natural phenomena and has had profound effects on patents in the life sciences, medical diagnostics, and personalized medicine. It sparked considerable debate about whether the test is too restrictive.

## Key Quotes
> "To transform an unpatentable law of nature into a patent-eligible application of such a law, one must do more than simply state the law of nature while adding the words 'apply it.'"
