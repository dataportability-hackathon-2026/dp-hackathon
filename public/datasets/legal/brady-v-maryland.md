# Brady v. Maryland

**Citation:** 373 U.S. 83 (1963)
**Year:** 1963
**Court:** Supreme Court of the United States
**Topic Area:** Criminal Procedure

## Facts
John Brady and a companion, Boblit, were convicted of first-degree murder in Maryland. Brady admitted his participation in the crime but claimed that Boblit had done the actual killing. Before trial, Brady's attorney requested to examine Boblit's extrajudicial statements. The prosecution withheld a statement in which Boblit admitted to the killing. Brady was convicted and sentenced to death. The withheld statement was discovered after trial.

## Issue
Whether the suppression by the prosecution of evidence favorable to an accused upon request violates due process where the evidence is material either to guilt or punishment.

## Holding
The Court held that the suppression by the prosecution of evidence favorable to the accused violates due process where the evidence is material either to guilt or to punishment, irrespective of the good faith or bad faith of the prosecution. The Court remanded the case for a new sentencing hearing, finding that the suppressed confession was material to the question of punishment but not guilt, since Brady had admitted his involvement.

## Significance
Brady established the prosecution's constitutional obligation to disclose exculpatory evidence to the defense, a requirement now known as the "Brady rule." The decision is fundamental to ensuring fair trials and has been expanded by subsequent cases to include impeachment evidence (Giglio v. United States) and evidence the prosecution should have known about. Brady violations remain one of the most common grounds for overturning convictions.

## Key Quotes
> "Society wins not only when the guilty are convicted but when criminal trials are fair; our system of the administration of justice suffers when any accused is treated unfairly."
