# District of Columbia v. Heller

**Citation:** 554 U.S. 570 (2008)
**Year:** 2008
**Court:** Supreme Court of the United States
**Topic Area:** Constitutional Law

## Facts
Dick Heller, a special police officer authorized to carry a handgun while on duty, applied for a registration certificate to keep a handgun at home. The District of Columbia denied his application under its Firearms Control Regulations Act of 1975, which effectively banned handgun possession by making it a crime to carry an unregistered firearm and prohibiting the registration of handguns. The law also required lawfully owned firearms to be kept unloaded and disassembled or trigger-locked.

## Issue
Whether the Second Amendment protects an individual's right to possess firearms independent of service in a state militia, and whether the District of Columbia's handgun ban and trigger-lock requirement violate that right.

## Holding
The Court held 5-4 that the Second Amendment protects an individual's right to possess a firearm unconnected with service in a militia, and to use that arm for traditionally lawful purposes such as self-defense within the home. The D.C. handgun ban and the trigger-lock requirement were unconstitutional because they made it impossible for citizens to use firearms for the lawful purpose of self-defense.

## Significance
Heller was the first Supreme Court case to hold that the Second Amendment protects an individual right to keep and bear arms. The decision settled a long-standing debate about whether the amendment protected only a collective right tied to militia service. It established that the right is not unlimited and that certain regulations remain permissible, but set the stage for future challenges to firearms regulations.

## Key Quotes
> "The Second Amendment protects an individual right to possess a firearm unconnected with service in a militia, and to use that arm for traditionally lawful purposes, such as self-defense within the home."
