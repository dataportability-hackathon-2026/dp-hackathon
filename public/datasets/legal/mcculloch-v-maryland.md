# McCulloch v. Maryland

**Citation:** 17 U.S. (4 Wheat.) 316 (1819)
**Year:** 1819
**Court:** Supreme Court of the United States
**Topic Area:** Constitutional Law

## Facts
Congress chartered the Second Bank of the United States in 1816. The state of Maryland imposed a tax on all banks not chartered by the state, which effectively targeted only the federal bank's Baltimore branch. James McCulloch, the cashier of the Baltimore branch, refused to pay the tax. Maryland sued, and the state courts ruled in Maryland's favor.

## Issue
Whether Congress has the power to incorporate a bank, and whether a state may tax an instrument of the federal government.

## Holding
Chief Justice Marshall held unanimously that Congress has the authority to establish a bank under the Necessary and Proper Clause, even though the Constitution does not explicitly grant that power. The Court also held that Maryland could not tax the federal bank, as the power to tax involves the power to destroy, and states cannot impede valid constitutional exercises of power by the federal government.

## Significance
McCulloch established the doctrine of implied powers, confirming that Congress may exercise powers not explicitly enumerated in the Constitution if they are necessary and proper for carrying out its enumerated powers. The decision also reinforced federal supremacy over state governments, setting a foundational precedent for the scope of congressional authority.

## Key Quotes
> "Let the end be legitimate, let it be within the scope of the Constitution, and all means which are appropriate, which are plainly adapted to that end, which are not prohibited, but consist with the letter and spirit of the Constitution, are constitutional."
