# Obergefell v. Hodges

**Citation:** 576 U.S. 644 (2015)
**Year:** 2015
**Court:** Supreme Court of the United States
**Topic Area:** Constitutional Law

## Facts
James Obergefell and John Arthur, a same-sex couple married in Maryland, sought recognition of their marriage in Ohio, which did not permit same-sex marriage. Arthur was terminally ill, and Obergefell wanted to be listed as the surviving spouse on the death certificate. This case was consolidated with cases from Michigan, Kentucky, and Tennessee, all challenging state bans on same-sex marriage.

## Issue
Whether the Fourteenth Amendment requires a state to license a marriage between two people of the same sex and to recognize a same-sex marriage licensed in another state.

## Holding
The Court held 5-4 that the fundamental right to marry is guaranteed to same-sex couples under the Due Process and Equal Protection Clauses of the Fourteenth Amendment. States must both license same-sex marriages and recognize those performed in other states. Justice Kennedy identified four principles supporting the conclusion: individual autonomy, the unique importance of the marital bond, safeguarding children, and marriage as a keystone of social order.

## Significance
Obergefell established marriage equality as the law of the land, invalidating same-sex marriage bans in the remaining fourteen states that had them. The decision represented a landmark moment in the civil rights movement for LGBTQ+ Americans and reshaped family law across the country. It built on decades of evolving jurisprudence regarding sexual orientation and equal protection.

## Key Quotes
> "No union is more profound than marriage, for it embodies the highest ideals of love, fidelity, devotion, sacrifice, and family."
