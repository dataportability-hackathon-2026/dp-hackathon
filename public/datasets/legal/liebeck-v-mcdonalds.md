# Liebeck v. McDonald's Restaurants

**Citation:** No. D-202 CV-93-02419 (N.M. Dist. 1994)
**Year:** 1994
**Court:** District Court of New Mexico
**Topic Area:** Torts

## Facts
Stella Liebeck, a 79-year-old woman, purchased a cup of coffee from a McDonald's drive-through in Albuquerque, New Mexico. While parked, she placed the cup between her knees to remove the lid and add cream. The coffee spilled, causing third-degree burns over 16 percent of her body, including her inner thighs, groin, and buttocks. She was hospitalized for eight days and required skin grafting. McDonald's served its coffee at 180-190 degrees Fahrenheit, significantly hotter than coffee served at home or by other restaurants.

## Issue
Whether McDonald's was liable for injuries caused by serving coffee at an unreasonably dangerous temperature, and whether the company's knowledge of prior burn complaints established negligence.

## Holding
The jury found McDonald's 80 percent at fault and Liebeck 20 percent at fault. It awarded $200,000 in compensatory damages, reduced to $160,000 for comparative fault, and $2.7 million in punitive damages. The judge reduced the punitive damages to $480,000. The parties ultimately settled for an undisclosed amount. The jury was persuaded by evidence that McDonald's had received over 700 prior burn complaints and had made a conscious decision not to lower its coffee temperature.

## Significance
Liebeck v. McDonald's became one of the most widely discussed tort cases in popular culture, often cited as an example of frivolous litigation. However, the actual facts revealed serious injuries and corporate indifference to known dangers. The case sparked national debate about tort reform, product liability standards, and the role of punitive damages in deterring corporate misconduct.

## Key Quotes
> "The quality of the product served was unreasonably dangerous and defective, and McDonald's had knowledge of the danger from previous complaints."
