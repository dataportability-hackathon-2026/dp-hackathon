# Strickland v. Washington

**Citation:** 466 U.S. 668 (1984)
**Year:** 1984
**Court:** Supreme Court of the United States
**Topic Area:** Criminal Procedure

## Facts
David Washington pleaded guilty to three counts of capital murder in Florida. Against his attorney's advice, he waived his right to an advisory jury during sentencing. His attorney, feeling hopeless about the case, chose not to seek out character witnesses, did not request a psychiatric examination, and did not investigate or present mitigating evidence. Washington was sentenced to death. He sought habeas corpus relief, claiming his Sixth Amendment right to effective counsel was violated.

## Issue
Whether the Sixth Amendment right to counsel guarantees not merely the presence of an attorney but effective assistance of counsel, and what standard should be used to evaluate claims of ineffective assistance.

## Holding
The Court established a two-part test for ineffective assistance of counsel claims. First, the defendant must show that counsel's performance was deficient, meaning it fell below an objective standard of reasonableness. Second, the defendant must show prejudice, meaning there is a reasonable probability that but for counsel's errors, the result of the proceeding would have been different. The Court found that Washington failed to satisfy either prong.

## Significance
The Strickland test remains the governing standard for evaluating ineffective assistance of counsel claims in virtually all criminal cases. While criticized by some as too deferential to defense attorneys and too difficult for defendants to satisfy, the framework has been applied thousands of times. It establishes a floor for attorney competence that protects defendants while preserving the independence of defense counsel's strategic judgments.

## Key Quotes
> "The benchmark for judging any claim of ineffectiveness must be whether counsel's conduct so undermined the proper functioning of the adversarial process that the trial cannot be relied on as having produced a just result."
