# Brown v. Board of Education

**Citation:** 347 U.S. 483 (1954)
**Year:** 1954
**Court:** Supreme Court of the United States
**Topic Area:** Constitutional Law

## Facts
Oliver Brown and other plaintiffs filed class action lawsuits against their local school boards, challenging state laws that established separate public schools for Black and white students. The cases were consolidated from Kansas, South Carolina, Virginia, and Delaware. The lower courts had upheld segregation based on the "separate but equal" doctrine from Plessy v. Ferguson (1896).

## Issue
Whether racial segregation of children in public schools violates the Equal Protection Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment, even when the physical facilities and other tangible factors are equal.

## Holding
The Court unanimously held that racial segregation in public schools is inherently unequal and therefore violates the Equal Protection Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment. The Court overruled Plessy v. Ferguson, finding that "separate but equal" has no place in the field of public education. Separating children solely on the basis of race generates a feeling of inferiority that may affect their hearts and minds in ways unlikely ever to be undone.

## Significance
Brown is one of the most significant Supreme Court decisions of the twentieth century. It dismantled the legal basis for racial segregation in schools and, by extension, challenged segregation in all public facilities. The decision became a catalyst for the civil rights movement and established that state-sponsored segregation is a violation of constitutional rights.

## Key Quotes
> "We conclude that, in the field of public education, the doctrine of 'separate but equal' has no place. Separate educational facilities are inherently unequal."
