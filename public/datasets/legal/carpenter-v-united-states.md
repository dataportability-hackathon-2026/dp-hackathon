# Carpenter v. United States

**Citation:** 585 U.S. 296 (2018)
**Year:** 2018
**Court:** Supreme Court of the United States
**Topic Area:** Data Privacy

## Facts
The FBI obtained cell-site location information (CSLI) from wireless carriers for Timothy Carpenter without a warrant, relying on the Stored Communications Act. The records covered 127 days and revealed 12,898 location points, placing Carpenter near several robbery locations. Carpenter moved to suppress the evidence, arguing it was obtained in violation of the Fourth Amendment.

## Issue
Whether the government's acquisition of historical cell-site location information from a wireless carrier constitutes a search under the Fourth Amendment requiring a warrant.

## Holding
The Court held 5-4 that accessing historical CSLI constitutes a search under the Fourth Amendment. The government must generally obtain a warrant supported by probable cause before acquiring such records. The Court declined to extend the third-party doctrine to cover the comprehensive, detailed, and effortlessly compiled record of a person's movements generated by cell phone location data.

## Significance
Carpenter marked a major evolution in Fourth Amendment jurisprudence for the digital age. The Court recognized that the pervasive and revealing nature of digital records requires greater privacy protections than traditional business records. The decision signaled that the third-party doctrine has limits when applied to the vast quantities of personal data generated by modern technology.

## Key Quotes
> "A cell phone faithfully follows its owner beyond public thoroughfares and into private residences, doctor's offices, political headquarters, and other potentially revealing locales."
